What it does

All of ffmpeg, none of the flags.

GoEncode applies one recipe to every file in the batch: video, audio, subtitles, chapters, metadata and output naming. It drives the ffmpeg and ffprobe programs you already have.

Every encoder ffmpeg has

At startup GoEncode reads what the installed ffmpeg can do (ffmpeg -codecs, plus -h encoder=… for each encoder) and offers all of it. The common ones — x264, x265, SVT‑AV1, libaom, rav1e, VP9, ProRes, DNxHR, FFV1, AAC, Opus, MP3, FLAC, AC‑3 and others — have curated settings.

Copy or remux

Copy any kind of stream untouched, so moving a file into a new container takes seconds and loses no quality.

Only valid choices

You only see codecs, pixel formats and sample rates that fit the current container and encoder. Change the format and settings that no longer apply are fixed for you.

Preview before you commit

Encode 5–60 seconds from any point. See the speed, the clip size, a projected size for the whole file, and the same frame from source and result side by side.

Explanations on hover

Every option has an ⓘ popover that explains what it does for the chosen codec.

Simple and advanced

Simple mode: one-click presets, a quality slider and a resolution. Advanced mode: every setting of every encoder, curated options first.

Blu-ray and DVD folders

A disc, or a copy of one, is added as one file: its main title, with its parts joined in order. Blu-ray tracks get their languages from the playlist.

Tracks per file

Keep all audio and subtitle tracks, the first, or those in chosen languages — or switch a file's tracks on and off one by one.

Batches that keep you posted

Drag and drop files or folders, encode up to 4 at once, follow per-file and overall progress, cancel or retry, and get a notification when it's done.

Simple mode

Twelve presets, one click each.

Start from a preset, then adjust quality and resolution — or switch to advanced mode for everything.

Bring your own ffmpeg

It finds ffmpeg for you.

GoEncode doesn't link ffmpeg; it looks for the programs in Preferences, next to the app, in common install folders, then on PATH. On Android, ffmpeg is packaged inside the APK.

# Windows
> winget install ffmpeg
# macOS
$ brew install ffmpeg
# Debian, Ubuntu
$ sudo apt install ffmpeg

Privacy policy

Your media stays on your device.

GoEncode collects no data. It has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting, and it doesn't connect to the internet: everything it does happens on your device. This policy covers the GoEncode app from GitHub, the Microsoft Store and Google Play.

What GoEncode sends

Nothing. Files are read and encoded by ffmpeg on your own device, and neither they nor anything about them is uploaded to the developer or anyone else. A file only leaves your device when you send it yourself, for example with the share sheet on Android, and then it goes to the app you choose.

What it keeps on your device

  • Preferences in GoEncode's app data folder: the ffmpeg location, theme, number of parallel encodes, whether to notify, and your last preset and settings.
  • Encoded files where you choose, next to their sources by default; on Android 11 and later in Download/GoEncode.
  • Preview clips in the system's temporary folder.
  • On Android, copies of the files you add, in the app's private storage, because Android hands apps a stream rather than a file. A copy is deleted when you remove the file from the batch.

To remove this data, delete GoEncode's app data folder; uninstalling does it for you on Android and in the Microsoft Store version. Encoded files are yours and stay where they were saved.

Permissions

  • Notifications (Android): to tell you when a batch has finished. You can turn this off in Preferences.
  • Running in the background (Android): a foreground service with a visible notification keeps a batch encoding while you use other apps.
  • Folders (macOS): macOS asks before GoEncode reads or writes in Documents, Desktop, Downloads or external drives, where your media may be.

Other parties

GoEncode runs ffmpeg as a local program; ffmpeg doesn't connect anywhere either. The store or website you download GoEncode from handles that download under its own privacy policy. GoEncode contains no third-party code that collects data.

Questions or concerns: open an issue on GitHub. If this policy changes, the new version is published here.
